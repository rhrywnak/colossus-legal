## The lawsuit

Marie Awad sues Catholic Family Services (CFS), a Michigan non-profit and a subdivision
of the Diocese of Saginaw, and George Phillips, the Michigan attorney who acted for CFS.
Charles "Chuck" Penzien is Marie's attorney. The suit was filed in Macomb County and
moved to the Bay County Circuit Court, where CFS and Phillips reside; it is now before
Chief Judge Joseph K. Sheeran. The complaint also named the Archdiocese of Detroit, which
has since been dropped from the case.

The complaint pleads four counts: breach of fiduciary duty, fraud, declaratory relief
(CFS acted as a fiduciary although its articles of incorporation never authorized that
business), and abuse of process. Each count claims damages exceeding $25,000, with
treble damages sought.

## Where it began — the guardianship and the $50,000

Marie is one of three daughters of Emil Awad, with her sisters Nadia Awad and Camille
Hanley. In August 2008, $50,000 Mr. Awad had deposited in his checking account was
removed the next day by Camille and never returned to him despite his repeated requests
— he wanted it for his health care. Camille eventually wrote him a $50,000 check, which
Nadia snatched from his hands.

A guardianship was filed over Mr. Awad in the Bay County Probate Court. Judge Tighe
ruled from the bench on April 21, 2009 that CFS would be guardian and conservator — over
the objection of Mr. Awad, a mentally competent adult, and despite his request that if
anyone be appointed it be Dr. Armaly, his longtime friend and a retired physician. It
was not revealed that CFS was under contract with the Bay County Probate Court as its
exclusive contractual guardian/conservator. No formal order of appointment was ever
entered. Mr. Awad died on May 4, 2009. CFS took possession of the $50,000 check and held it
two and a half months with no court order and no appointment — its first actual
appointment came on July 23, 2009, when Judge Tighe made it personal representative of
the estate.

## The administration of the estate

Marie brought her concerns to the personal representative: the taking of the $50,000,
estate fraud, an attempted extortion of $500,000 from her father, attempted Medicaid
fraud, and the involvement of Attorney Milton Higgs in her sisters' conduct. The
complaint alleges that on receiving this, Phillips began a pattern of spurious
accusations against Marie to discredit and silence her, including:

- telling the court, in a bankruptcy matter he knew otherwise about, that a loan from
  her father had been discharged, when it had been reaffirmed and was two-thirds repaid;
- calling a video Marie and her father made "pathetic; a hostage situation; something
  out of North Korea" — while knowing Mr. Awad had said the same things privately to the
  guardian ad litem and his home health professionals, whose sworn affidavits were given
  to the defendants;
- calling her positions "unintelligible," "fanciful conspiracy theories," unmeritorious
  and baseless, without taking testimony and while ignoring the sworn evidence;
- holding an auction of property the defendants themselves called "worthless" and
  "junk," after their first auctioneer withdrew for lack of value, justified by the
  claim Marie could not act amicably — though she had agreed by certified letter to
  divide the property amicably. The estate lost about $6,000 on the auction while CFS
  and Phillips billed about $7,500; the unsold property was then divided amicably by
  the sisters;
- telling the court Marie's changes of attorney caused most of the estate's $30,000
  administrative cost, when the defendants' own billing shows at most about $500;
- recommending payment of her sisters' claim for the whole funeral bill while ignoring
  Marie's bank-record proof that joint assets of all three sisters had paid it;
- seeking sanctions against Marie when her attorney missed filing an appearance, while
  her sister's attorney made the same error without any such request.

The estate settlement order was entered October 14, 2010 without testimony or evidence,
with the majority of the estate going to CFS and its counsel as fees. Marie appealed on
November 2, 2010; the Court of Appeals issued its opinion January 12, 2012. The
defendants then had the costs of that appeal assessed against Marie alone, by order of
April 12, 2012.

After the appeal, Marie learned of assets never brought into the estate: at least one
Social Security payment and two PNC accounts. She informed the defendants; the
complaint alleges they intended to abandon those funds even though the Social Security
Administration advised her they were available to the personal representative.

## Developments after the complaint (from the sworn record)

In discovery under oath in 2016, Phillips said his statements about Marie rested on
"comments made by other interested parties," named no one in particular, and called his
characterizations of her his "personal conclusions." In 2012 CFS had filed a form with
the Social Security Administration, concerning a payment of about $1,247, stating Mr.
Awad had no living children — while dealing with all three daughters. Marie first
learned that form existed when the SSA sent her a copy in a letter dated late November
2025, in her hands in early December 2025; CFS had not mentioned it when asked under
oath in 2016 about the Social Security money.

In April 2025, ruling on Marie's motions for default and summary disposition, Chief
Judge Sheeran denied both — questions of fact are for trial — but wrote that the number
of contradictory discovery responses was "troubling," that the defendants' responses
"could justifiably be regarded as cursory or lacking at best and intentionally evasive
at worst," and that the Court "will reserve these issues to be addressed at the
conclusion of Trial." Among the contradictions the opinion recites: CFS said in one
response it had provided the mover correspondence and in another that it did not have
it; CFS said no auction payment ledger exists while Phillips's response indicates a
list does exist; on title documents, CFS said the documents "speak for themselves"
while Phillips said he could not provide any. The case now moves to trial.
